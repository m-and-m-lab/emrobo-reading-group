The idea

We want to understand how representations of the robot's body appear, or fail to appear, in current robot learning work on physical action and interaction: what gets represented, how that representation is used or adapted, and what capabilities emerge when embodiment is modeled as first-class structure rather than absorbed implicitly through data, heuristics or modular handoffs.

Recent works such as Body Transformer and GET-Zero encode robot morphology and connectivity directly in the architecture. In parallel, planning work such as the Virtual Kinematic Chain and sequential mobile manipulation planning unifies base, arm and scene kinematics, and shows where splitting mobile manipulation into separate locomotion and manipulation modules can lose feasibility or robustness.

The community is moving from robot-specific policies toward cross-embodiment learning. We'd like to push that conversation beyond transfer itself, toward how the choice of embodiment representation affects downstream reasoning, planning, control and interaction. A related direction is embodied skill learning and skill representation, particularly for mobile and dexterous manipulation: how skills can be represented, transferred or adapted across different bodies, instead of staying tied to one platform or one low-level action space.

What we mean by embodiment

Broadly, the physical properties and constraints that shape how a robot can act and interact with the world.

In general
Morphology, kinematics, dynamics, contacts, sensing, actuation limits, controller interfaces, latency, stability, and other hardware-dependent constraints.
In mobile manipulation
Base–arm kinematics and nonholonomy, reachability and manipulability, sensor placement and occlusion, contact modes, and whole-body stability.
Off nominal
Actuator degradation, payload changes, sensor noise, damage, and changing contact conditions. Adapting to these depends on some meaningful representation of what the robot can currently do.

Why this area

Aligning different robots starts with the body

Methods that work on one platform often transfer poorly because embodiment is never represented, even when the task looks the same. Making it explicit, as Body Transformer and GET-Zero do, clarifies what to share across robots, what stays robot-specific, and what to adapt online.

The right plan depends on the robot

The same task may need a different base placement, viewpoint, grasp or motion sequence on a different robot. VKC and SMMP show that modeling these coupled constraints exposes actions that separate navigation and manipulation modules miss. Can learned systems get the same benefit?

Adaptation needs a model of current ability

Actuators degrade, payloads change, sensors drift and contacts vary. Should a representation describe only the nominal robot, or its changing effective capabilities? This follows the internal–external framing in our earlier embodiment work.

Skills add a second representation

Joint-space skills are tied to one robot; very abstract ones can lose the physical constraints needed to execute them. We want skill representations, including UniSkill-style ones, that transfer without forgetting the body.

No one agrees what a useful representation is

Kinematic graphs, morphology tokens, inertial parameters, latent embeddings, contact history, robot IDs: the field uses all of them. Comparing them side by side, and seeing what each loses, is the point of the group.

What we look for

We don't expect any method to have all of these. Part of the point is to see which capabilities current representations support well and where the gaps are.

  • Generalization across embodiments with minimal robot-specific tuning
  • Adaptation to changes in the robot's physical capabilities
  • Support for contact-rich manipulation and locomotion
  • Support for long-horizon and whole-body tasks
  • Transferable or reusable skill representations
  • Clear sim-to-real transfer
  • Interpretable links between the body representation and behavior
  • Evaluations that separate robot, sensor, morphology and dynamics changes
  • Downstream improvements attributable to the representation itself
