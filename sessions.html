How sessions work

Each session focuses on one main paper or a small set of closely related papers. The presenter follows a shared template and keeps the talk focused, so there are 15–20 minutes for discussion. Sessions run an hour, every other Tuesday.

Once a month there's an extra session on the off week, the Tooling Hour: a hands-on walkthrough of a tool, codebase, simulator or hardware setup.

Everyone can join in person or on Zoom. With the consent of presenters and participants, selected sessions may be recorded and posted to the group's YouTube channel.

Presenting

Cover these points, in roughly this order, and leave the last 15–20 minutes for discussion. Points 3, 4, 7 and 8 are the B·T·L·E questions.

  1. What is the problem setting, and what are the assumptions?
  2. What is the core idea, in one sentence?
  3. What representation of embodiment or skill is used?
  4. How is that representation integrated into the method?
  5. What is new compared with prior work?
  6. How should we understand the method from our own perspective?
  7. What downstream capability does the representation enable?
  8. What is the evaluation setup: metrics, environments and embodiments?
  9. Two strengths and two weaknesses.
  10. Reproducibility: code, data, simulator and hardware requirements.
  11. One extension relevant to your lab's work or your own interests.

Before and after

  • Two weeks before: pick a paper from the reading list and confirm the date with an organizer.
  • One week before: send the paper link and any paired reading, so it can go out in Slack.
  • On the day: bring two or three open questions to start the discussion.
  • Afterwards: share your slides, and say whether you're happy for the recording to be posted.
