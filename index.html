What we study

Representing the body, and what it enables

About the group →

Many robot learning methods treat the robot's body, sensors, action space and physical constraints as a fixed part of the experimental setup. We read the work that represents them explicitly, from kinematic graphs and morphology-aware policies to robot tokens, tactile embeddings and embodiment-aware world models. We also ask where those representations still fall short.

01

Representation

What form does the embodiment representation take, and what does the model actually know about the body?

02

Adaptation

How newer methods build on, modify or replace earlier representations, and why.

03

Skills

How embodied skills are represented, learned, composed and transferred, especially for mobile and dexterous manipulation.

04

Limitations

What each representation cannot capture, and where it breaks when hardware, tasks or conditions change.

Weekly

One main paper or a small set of closely related papers. A 60-minute presentation, then 30 minutes of discussion and Q&A.

1–2 hours

Practical and informal. Robot assets (URDF/USD), Isaac Sim and Isaac Lab workflows, sensing hardware, datasets and lessons learned from implementation.

60 min
